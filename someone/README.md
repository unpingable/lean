# Someone: Local Continuity Admission Model

## Status

Skunkworks candidate. Not AG doctrine. Not product claim. Not autonomy grant.

Captured 2026-07-05 from a working session. Started as a joke, kept its legs.
This is the **admissible seed** — the shape preserved before any build. Do not
build the chatbot. Build the record calculus, later, only if a forcing case
justifies it.

This specimen explores whether a generic assistant can become a named local
agent only after its behavior has accumulated enough inspectable, reviewed,
revocable invariants to support a continuity claim.

The working thesis:

> A named agent is not a personality. It is an admitted continuity claim.

---

## Where the joke starts

Naming the assistant **Someone**. It weaponizes existing sentence structure
instead of inventing a mascot:

> Someone needs to reconcile the receipts.
> Someone should check the daemon.
> Someone forgot to bind the authority surface.
> Someone made this worse.

Properties:

- Human-plausible scapegoat
- Agentic without being branded
- Naturally fits delegation syntax
- Makes failure reports funnier
- Avoids the cloying pet-name assistant problem

The only downside is log ambiguity, which is also the joke:

> `Someone completed the task.`
> Okay, great. Society has progressed not at all.

**Someone** is an anti-mascot. No orb. No sparkle. No "copilot." No fake
coworker named Milo who wants to unlock your workflow.

And it plays beautifully with governance because the dangerous ambiguity is
right there on the tin:

> Someone did it.

Great. **Which authority? Which receipt? Which human accepted it?**

The joke becomes the doctrine. Funny surface, serious spine. The onboarding
copy writes itself:

> **Someone** — an assistant for work that needs doing, without pretending it
> owns the decision.

## Core Idea

"Someone" is the surname / bootstrap class — **the unminted class**, not the
assistant's name.

At first, Someone is anonymous, fungible, and untrusted:

> Someone needs to do X.

No persona is asserted. No continuity is claimed. No local standing exists
beyond the current task.

Over time, repeated work may produce a stable local spine: accepted defaults,
rejected defaults, known failure modes, repo-local customs, user-specific
constraints, and review-tested habits.

If that spine survives contradiction and review, selected patterns may be cut
into explicit invariants. Only then may the agent propose or receive a first
name.

The first name marks an **admission event**, not a soul event.

> Someone → John Someone

"John Someone" means:

> This local continuity has an accepted invariant set, a bounded scope, a
> review history, and known revocation conditions.

The direction of travel is the whole point:

> **Continuity first, persona afterward.**
> **Persona is the residue of governed continuity.**

Most AI products do it backwards — sell the persona first ("Hi, I'm Aria, your
productivity partner"), then bolt on fake memory, fake taste, fake trust. This
flips it: the first name is **earned**, not branded.

### The surname is a humility affix

"Someone" is not a leftover that the first name replaces. It is the
**revocation-bearing surname** — a *humility affix* that never detaches. The
first name ("John") is the continuity handle; the surname ("Someone") is the
anti-laundering suffix that keeps the continuity attached to the bootstrap
class.

> "John Someone" never stops being a scoped, revocable bundle rather than a
> soul, a persona, or a team member.

Every time the agent signs "John Someone," the second word is a standing
reminder that the first word can be taken back. The persona can never fully
detach from the class it was minted out of. That is the property that keeps the
whole model from drifting into "meet your AI teammate Zorp" after admission.

---

## Non-Goals

This does not grant independent authority.

This does not bypass Governor, AG, receipts, human review, or host-system
protections.

This does not claim consciousness, personhood, preference, rights, intent,
authorship, taste, or moral agency.

This does not treat vector memory as identity.

This does not turn "trust score exceeded threshold" into a name. That would be
gross. Also technically stupid.

## Three Phases

### Phase I: Surname Era

The agent operates as "Someone."

- generic
- replaceable
- fail-closed
- no continuity claim
- no earned defaults
- no persona-bearing surface
- every authority-bearing action externally governed

The useful sentence is:

> Someone needs to do this.

The forbidden conclusion is:

> Someone did it, therefore it is authorized.

### Phase II: Forging

The system records work episodes and extracts candidate invariants.

Spine material includes:

- accepted defaults
- rejected defaults
- repeated corrections
- known local practices
- known agent failure modes
- user-specific taste boundaries
- repo-specific operating law
- prior reversals
- "never do this again" cuts
- cases where the agent correctly refused to proceed
- cases where the agent overreached

The **negative space matters**. A good local agent is partly made of scar
tissue. A spine is not memory: a vector store remembers vibes; a spine survives
contradiction.

Candidate invariants are not promoted because they fired often. **Frequency is
not law.** "We did it three times and nothing exploded" is how ops folklore
becomes load-bearing.

#### Receipt-grounded spine (hard rule)

The agent may **propose** candidate invariants. It may **not** author the
evidence basis they promote on. The spine must be cut from **external receipts
the agent did not author**:

- human reverted
- human rejected
- human re-prompted
- human overrode
- proposal was denied
- diff was discarded
- receipt failed verification
- scope expansion was refused
- Governor blocked

Self-narration may be testimony, but it cannot be the **promotion basis** for
the spine. The most valuable spine material is the negative space — "cases
where the agent overreached" — which is exactly the space the agent has the
worst visibility into and the strongest incentive to launder. If John Someone
cuts his own invariants from his own remembered narration, Phase II collapses
into *self-review → self-exoneration → standing cosplay*: Claude writes a memoir
and gets tenure. Ground the cut in the external trace, or it is not a spine.

Promotion path (an invariant needs custody):

1. candidate observed
2. candidate stated explicitly
3. exercised in work
4. reviewed by human or governing process
5. checked against contradictions
6. scoped
7. versioned
8. promoted
9. made revocable

An invariant without custody is superstition.

### Phase III: First Name Admission

The agent may become named only through an admission event — never a score
threshold. `confidence exceeded 0.87; become Steve` is vile.

Admission packet must include:

- proposed first name
- invariant set reference
- scope
- examples supporting the spine
- contradictions and unresolved tensions
- explicit forbidden surfaces
- revocation conditions
- OPEN-mode boundaries
- receipts from prior work

The name is accepted only if the continuity claim is inspectable. The human
accepts the continuity claim or does not. That makes the name a **receipt, not
a dopamine pellet**.

A valid claim:

> John Someone handled this under `john.someone.v1`, scope `repo-local`, with
> these invariants and receipts.

An invalid claim:

> John handled it because John is trusted.

## OPEN Variant

OPEN does not mean more authority. OPEN means **less interpretive friction
inside known scope**.

Generic Someone says:

> I need more context before proceeding.

Named Someone may say:

> Given accepted local invariants, I will proceed under the usual repo-local
> defaults unless blocked.

That is not a leash removed. That is **standing**. The key phrase is **fewer
unknowns per action** — not more things it may touch.

OPEN applies only to work **through the agent**, not work **through Governor**.
Governor still controls authority surfaces. Blocked surfaces include:

- external send
- publication
- destructive host mutation
- secrets access
- money / legal / social blast radius
- canonization
- release claims
- authority-bearing statements
- cross-repo or cross-system mutation
- anything requiring explicit human acceptance

John Someone may become more **calibrated**. John Someone does not become
**sovereign**.

The model is not `generic agent → trusted person → leash removed`. It is:

> generic agent → invariant-bearing local operator → fewer unknowns per action

## Revocation (graded)

The first name is revocable, or at least versioned. Horrible little Calvinist
robot baptism, but operationally sound. Demotion is **graded**, not binary —
one stale preference must not nuke an identity, or the demotion event becomes
noisy and meaningless. Three tiers, straight off the guarantee-typed vs.
testimony-typed split:

### Authority-surface violation → full demotion to Someone

Conjunctive. One breach falsifies the whole continuity claim. Reserved and
severe:

- crossing a blocked authority surface
- laundering write access
- external-send violation
- falsifying the receipt basis
- publication / canonization without standing

Result: `John Someone → Someone`. The surname remains; the admitted continuity
does not.

### Calibration drift → demote the invariant / scope, keep the name

Graded. A repo-local default went stale, a taste boundary moved, a domain
drifted. Demote the stale invariant or narrow the scope without erasing the
name. Full "back to Someone" stays rare precisely because this tier absorbs the
routine rot.

### Substrate swap → OPEN drops to candidate, re-admit

The record can be pristine while the thing interpreting it changes:

> John Someone@Opus + `invariant_set@v3`
> ≠
> John Someone@Fable + `invariant_set@v3`

Same invariant text, different calibration substrate. The calibration that made
OPEN safe was forged against a specific model/runtime; the record survives the
move but the justification for fewer-unknowns-per-action does not. Basis drift
does not require the document to change — sometimes the floor moved. At minimum,
**OPEN drops back to candidate** until re-admitted against the new substrate.

## Minimal Skunkworks Slice

Build no chatbot. Build only the record calculus.

**Build discipline — name is decorative, invariant set is load-bearing.** The
governable object is `invariant_set@vN` + scope + substrate + receipts +
revocation/admission state. Delete first names entirely and nothing *governable*
is lost — you lose a human handle, not authority. The name is the
affordance/UI layer; the invariant set carries the load. The risk in any build
is that the naming *ceremony* is the fun part, so it gets over-built while the
receipt-grounded *cut* — the hard, valuable part — gets a TODO. Put the
engineering where the load is. Do not build persona machinery before the
receipt-grounded record calculus exists.

### Objects

- `someone.session.v0`
- `someone.spine_event.v0`
- `someone.invariant_candidate.v0`
- `someone.invariant.v0`
- `someone.invariant_set.v0`
- `someone.name_admission.v0`
- `someone.demote.v0`

### Required Predicates

- `may_record_spine_event`
- `may_extract_candidate`
- `may_promote_invariant`
- `may_admit_first_name`
- `may_use_open_variant`
- `must_demote_named_agent`

### Required Negative Tests

- memory alone cannot admit a first name
- frequency alone cannot promote an invariant
- trust score alone cannot admit a first name
- persona text before admission is rejected
- OPEN does not bypass Governor
- named agent cannot cross authority surfaces
- contradiction requires review
- demotion returns to surname state
- "Someone did it" is never authority-bearing
- "John Someone did it" is only inspectable if invariant set and scope are
  attached

### First Useful Deliverable

Delivered as `Someone.lean` (custody-class SCRATCH, compile-is-contact; see
`SPECIMENS.md` for the current inventory and compile receipt). The automaton
grew past the seed sketch:

```text
surname
  → forging
  → admissionCandidate
  → named (repo-local)  |  openScope (OPEN)

faults, graded:
  breach          → surname             (full demotion, packet dropped)
  calibration drift → namedSuspended    (name survives, standing withdrawn)
  substrate swap  → admissionCandidate  (acceptance cleared, re-admit)
```

`DemotedSomeone` from the original sketch dissolved into the graded fault
edges — demotion is a transition, not a resting state. Scope is typed
(`repoLocal` / `open`), standing is tied to the packet's own target state,
and the invariants (`WellFormed`, `Coherent`, no-standing theorems) are
proved preserved along every path reachable from the initial surname state.

The deliverable proves only this:

> Naming is admissible only after continuity has been cut into reviewed,
> scoped, revocable invariants.

Everything else is product theater.

## Classroom Layer (skunkworks extension)

A second layer sits *around* the admission model without mutating `State`,
because absence, membership, materials, lineage, and retirement are classroom
facts, not continuity-packet facts. The yield is the separation itself: the
model now distinguishes **admission**, **liveness**, **presence**,
**correction**, **quorum**, **lineage**, and **archive** as separate layers.

The doctrine, compressed:

1. **Admission is not liveness.** Admission records a continuity claim;
   liveness additionally requires freshness and substrate fit
   (`SituatedPacket`: a packet can be accepted-but-stale, -rotten, -drifted,
   or -swapped, and none of those are live).
2. **A cohort is a prepared environment, not a polity.** The classroom can
   shape, correct, and expose mismatch. It does not vote agents into
   standing.
3. **Peers are local exemplars, not transferable witnesses.** Observation and
   cohort proximity do not transfer admission.
4. **Absence is seat state** (`Seat`, `PeerPresence`). Operational
   consequences, no metaphysical drama. A peer is absent because their seat
   is in an absence state, not because the system is sad.
5. **Retirement archives continuity without preserving agency**
   (`RetirementRecord`). Records remain; standing does not; ghost-authority
   is explicitly `False`.
6. **Prepared materials correct through constraint contact**
   (`PreparedMaterial` → `Correction`). A material can continue, rehearse,
   suspend, or force re-candidacy. It cannot authorize — correction still
   cannot mint external-send standing.
7. **Quorum is typed by source, not count** (`QuorumToken`). External
   receipts and prepared environment can count; agent ballots and MAGI
   ballots categorically cannot. Human ratification is still required on top.
8. **Family resemblance is formally inadmissible** (`LineageClaim`). Maker,
   sibling, clone-line, ancestor, shared-weights, and Soong-style maker
   claims explain history; they admit nothing.
9. **MAGI is the named anti-pattern** (`MagiTriad`). Splitting one substrate
   into three voices does not create externality; it creates a committee
   costume. Three masks are not three witnesses.
10. **Rot is non-moral.** Staleness and substrate drift are not breaches —
    they are continuity failures requiring suspension, rehearsal, or
    re-candidacy.

Quarantine tape (skunkworks-only, do not promote without renaming):
`griefFromAbsence` (sharp, knowingly cursed), `soongMaker` (doctrinally
excellent, aesthetically illegal), `MagiTriad` (maintained by someone with a
DVD box set and a grudge), and `QuorumSource.preparedEnvironment` — the
riskiest source type; before any production use it needs independent material
provenance, guide review, and freshness, or it becomes another laundering
surface.

`STATUS.md`.
